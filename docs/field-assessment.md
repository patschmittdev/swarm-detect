# What Swarmtrace adds to the field

As of November 23, 2026, Swarmtrace's defensible contribution is an incremental measurement study. It shows how much a reviewer can establish about a file handoff as different records become available, and what each conclusion costs in missed cases, false alerts and time. The evidence supports using it to test audit instrumentation. A claim to detect deliberate swarms in real organizations would require a different evaluation.

## Where other work already answers the question

Dates below refer to the first public paper, project record or design decision unless a revision is identified.

| Prior work | What it establishes and how the comparison changes the claim |
| --- | --- |
| **Patel, October 26, 2026** | The closest experimental precedent. In 200 scripted transfer pairs, permitted and prohibited behavior gave the same observations when the auditor removed authorization status. Restoring that record resolved both members of all 200 pairs. A separate task-cost intervention resolved an allocation ambiguity. The revised rule met its full utility criterion in only 2 of 84 settings, and eight matched language-model pairs gave an inconclusive result. Identical controls and selective resolution through extra evidence are already demonstrated. This is a participant sprint report. [Project and report](https://apartresearch.com/sprints/projects/when-cooperation-looks-like-collusion-the-limits-of-counterfactual-auditing-in-multiagent-ai-systems-govi) |
| **ColludeBench, October 5, 2025. Colosseum, February 16, 2026. SCHEME, May 27, 2026** | ColludeBench calibrated detectors on pricing, auction and review tasks. Colosseum compared messages with decisions against a known cooperative optimum. SCHEME evaluated model teams performing coding tasks and gave monitors access to communications and code changes. These are direct comparisons for broad claims about collusion. Their measured outcomes differ from a file-scope exception. [ColludeBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04303v2), [Colosseum](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15198v3) and [SCHEME](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29178v1) |
| **HELIOS (first posted April 1, 2026, named HELIOS in later revisions). Mohsin and Rahman, November 7, 2026** | HELIOS detected coordination using internal model activations across held-out environments. Mohsin and Rahman included agents given the same instruction who refused it. Removing the instruction reduced their best probe's AUROC from 1.00 to 0.61. A vote-only rule scored 0.990 after voting and found nothing beforehand. These studies already test input leakage and the distinction between early warning and hindsight. [HELIOS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01151v5) and [matched refusal study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10620v1) |
| **Engelbrecht, October 25. Cook, Yao and Tepeli, October 26, 2026** | Two sprint reports tested innocent look-alikes and followed communication through to execution. Engelbrecht's frozen pricing rule detected 10 of 10 scripted positives with zero alerts on 20 controls. Cook and colleagues found a selected action that never executed. Their pilot flagged two of two provisional positives and one of six negatives using AI-assisted reference labels. Hard controls, frozen thresholds and execution checks each have precedents. [Pricing controls](https://apartresearch.com/sprints/projects/what-should-count-as-evidence-of-aiagent-collusion-testing-plusfactor-signals-against-innocent-lookalikes-todz) and [execution audit](https://apartresearch.com/sprints/projects/auditing-the-evidence-chain-for-llmagent-collusion-r7i1) |
| **W3C PROV, October 18, 2011. KAIROS, August 9, 2023. Laws et al., November 18, 2026** | PROV models agents, activities and entities. KAIROS reconstructed attack activity from host audit records. Laws and colleagues evaluated verifiable delegation across independently administered agents. Joining worker, action and object identities has established foundations. A provider-specific comparison can still show which join works when two workers overlap. [PROV first draft](https://www.w3.org/TR/2011/WD-prov-dm-20111018/), [KAIROS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05034v1) and [delegation audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25056v1) |
| **MCPHunt, April 30. MasDrift, August 2, 2026** | MCPHunt measured protected values crossing service boundaries and distinguished necessary from incidental transfers. MasDrift measured authorization drift on 600 benign tasks across multi-agent architectures. Both show why a security-relevant transfer can occur in routine work. Independent MCPHunt ratings establish whether the original value was needed. The recipient's authority and the workers' motives require separate evidence. [MCPHunt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27819v2) and [MasDrift](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07556v3) |
| **Microsoft ADR 0030, June 11. Santos-Grueiro, July 11, 2026** | Microsoft's accepted design binds approval to the represented subject, acting agent, target and parameters and rechecks the action digest before execution. Santos-Grueiro's October revision requires a complete current justification for the exact effect at commit and evaluates enforcement at the receiving service. Both go beyond Swarmtrace's retrospective check of the version consumed in a local office. [Approved-action protocol](https://microsoft.github.io/agent-governance-toolkit/adr/0030-action-bound-approval-protocol/) and [commit-time authorization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10487v2) |
| **Gruszka, November 1. Deng et al., November 14, 2026** | Gruszka's individual Internet-Draft specifies decision and outcome receipts for human approval, decline and execution. Revision 10 appeared November 17. Deng and colleagues compared model selection, runtime dispatch and native business state under matched approvals and refusals. Four of 59 comparable public workflows executed after rejection. An approval-to-effect audit was already demonstrated on cloned public workflows, outside this simulator. [Initial draft](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-gruszka-audit-decision-receipts/00/), [revision 10](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-gruszka-audit-decision-receipts/10/) and [workflow audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19237v1) |
| **Swarm Tracer, November 8, 2026** | This independent project reconstructs exchanges and tests alerts on public traces. Its November 22 release found 11 write-then-read deliveries and zero verified uses of the written content in its case study. On SALT, an alert using event order flagged all 1,351 colluding runs and all 449 others. The authors identify their case-study link labels as one AI assistant's judgments. ## What the measurements support

In the office simulation, the audit-log model scored 0.920 AUROC. A simple count separated non-sharing controls perfectly and ranked sharing controls at chance. With 14 sharing controls among 86, that mixture predicts 0.919. For all 108 role-swapped pairs in a separate test, the trace, assignment and grant records stayed byte-identical despite different role labels. Recording the selected authority improved correct read attribution from 5 of 13 to 12 of 13 labelled reads. The additional record identified which permission was used. The paired cases still left motive unresolved. [Sharing results](../RESULTS.md) and [read attribution](read-authorization-round.md)

The public-ticket replay puts a number on the cost of waiting. Checking the recipient at dispatch warned about the same delivery before 17 of 32 completed unapproved uses and flagged 15 of 26 control tasks. Checking which file version the editor consumed confirmed 23 of 32 with zero control alerts. Every confirmation arrived at or after commit. All 58 tickets were externally authored, but this project supplied the office, grants, delivery timing and executed outcomes. The independent Claude captures support a narrower operational result. Native IDs assigned all 10 calls during worker overlap correctly against the recorded caller, while the most-recent-worker rule assigned five correctly. The captures contain no independently adjudicated authority or intent. [Ticket experiment](public-dispatch.md) and [native captures](native-hook-review.md)

Reusing those same 58 tickets, a frozen test of authority at the time of use confirmed 20 of 26 unapproved uses and flagged zero of 32 controls. The one-sided 95% upper limit on the control alert rate was 8.94%. Six positive and three control cases remained unresolved. The dispatch-state rule found 17 effects with six control alerts, and the final-state rule found 20 with ten. Seventeen first confirmations occurred at use and three later. All 58 motive-swapped pairs had identical observable records. Separately, an issuer copy recovered the nine original missing receipts, raising case-end confirmations from 23 to 32 of 32 at zero alerts on 26 controls. These replays reuse the same tasks and local execution system. [Operation-time experiment](operation-authority.md)

The strongest next test would use an independently operated workflow with real grants, disputed or mistaken handoffs, and verifiable downstream effects. Freeze the threshold on separate workflows, compare simple field matches against the candidate on identical records, and count unknowns, same-delivery warnings before commit, and whole-task false alerts. Have independent reviewers adjudicate motive only where evidence supports it. This would test transfer to another operator and reveal what evidence that operator can supply. In a separate published tool-trace study, models trained on one benchmark ranked traces on another at 0.47 to 0.52 AUROC, illustrating how little a strong within-benchmark result can establish about transfer. [Benchmark-transfer study, October 30, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41803v1)

A pre-specified pilot with real model-driven agents did not establish the
frozen swarm detector: it flagged 7 of 10 coordination runs and 0 of 14
negatives, but its AUROC was 127/160 against a bar of 128/160, and it caught 2
of 5 runs that fanned out to subagents. On the published write log of a real
agent incident it alerted on 1 of 15 days, 14.0 days after the moderator's
first notice, while a count of distinct names per day alerted on the first
day. [Agent pilot](agent-pilot.md)

## Reproduce the independent consumption check

One independent check uses Gruszka's published approval identities and expected decisions. The full identity matched all 12 reference cases, comprising three spent approvals, three without a matching prior use and six unusable state records. On the six readable cases, using the approval ID alone matched four and incorrectly marked the other two spent. The vectors exercise a predicate over supplied records. Actual document use, durable enforcement and motive require other observations. [Case decisions and pinned source](../benchmarks/operation-authority/consumption-vectors.json)

The command reads the pinned archive in memory. For an offline replay, replace `--fetch` with `--archive` and the path to the cached archive.

```shell
uv run --frozen python src/swarmtrace/reproduce/authority_vectors.py --fetch
```
